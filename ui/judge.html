Crowd crushes build for minutes, in known places, before anyone dies.

Every person is detected and tracked on the GPU by a PipeGen pipeline.

RequirementPipeline IRModelCompiler → TensorRTRun

CrowdShield runs no neural network of its own. It reads PipeGen's per-frame JSON (boxes, tracker IDs, timestamps) and turns it into decisions.

Density, the flow of the crowd, and the pressure that comes before a crush.

ρ(r) = 1/(πR²) · Σ exp(−|rⱼ − r|² / R²)

V(r) = Σ wⱼ vⱼ / Σ wⱼ

P(r) = ρ(r) · Vart[V(r)]

People are mapped to the floor in metres through a calibrated homography. Velocity comes from tracker IDs over at least one second. When local pressure stays above its threshold the motion is turbulent. Johansson, Helbing et al. (2008) observed this about 10 minutes before the 2006 Mina disaster.

The numbers, and the files they come from.

What CrowdShield is not.

  • It advises trained staff. It never opens or closes a gate.
  • One fixed camera gives a 2D view. Occlusion in dense crowds hides people, so density is corrected by a recall curve measured on the camera.
  • Thresholds are literature starting points, calibrated on this camera. They are not validated safety limits.
  • Simulated, synthetic and replayed data are always labelled as such.
  • No video or faces are stored, only positions and metrics.
